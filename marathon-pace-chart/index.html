Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good marathon time for a beginner?

For a first marathon, finishing under 5 hours is a solid goal that requires a pace of 11:27/mile (7:06/km). Most first-time marathoners finish between 4:30–5:30. A sub-4:00 marathon is considered a strong performance, requiring a 9:09/mile (5:41/km) pace — achievable after 2–3 years of consistent training.

What pace do I need for a sub-4 hour marathon?

To run a marathon in under 4 hours you need to maintain a pace of 9:09 per mile or 5:41 per km throughout the entire 26.2 miles. This means every single mile, including the tough final 10K, must be at or under 9:09. Most runners who achieve this have a 5K time under 24 minutes and a half marathon under 1:50.

What is a Boston Qualifier (BQ) marathon time?

Boston Qualifier times vary by age group and gender. Men 18–34 need sub-3:00 (6:52/mile pace); women 18–34 need sub-3:30 (8:01/mile). Times get progressively more lenient with age — men 60–64 qualify at 3:50 and women at 4:20. Note that actual cutoff times are typically 2–6 minutes faster than the official standards due to demand.

How do I pace a marathon correctly?

The most common strategy is even splits — running the same pace throughout. Many elite runners use negative splits (running the second half slightly faster). Avoid going out too fast in the first 10K; most marathon blow-ups happen because of the first 5 miles being run 20–30 seconds per mile too fast. Aim to reach the halfway point feeling like you've only run a hard tempo effort.

What marathon time should I expect from my half marathon PR?

A common rule of thumb: double your half marathon time and add 10–20 minutes. For example, a 1:50 half marathon suggests a marathon around 3:50–4:00. More precisely, multiply your half marathon time by 2.1. This assumes consistent marathon-specific long run training (20+ mile long runs, multiple 18–20 mile runs).

What is the average marathon time?

The average marathon finish time worldwide is approximately 4:32 for men and 4:56 for women, based on large race datasets. At 4:32, men average about 10:23/mile (6:27/km). These figures have gradually increased over decades as more first-time runners participate in the sport.
