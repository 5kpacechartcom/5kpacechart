How do I calculate my 5K pace?

Divide your total 5K finish time (in seconds) by 3.10686 (miles) for pace per mile, or by 5 (km) for pace per km. For example, a 25:00 finish = 25×60 ÷ 3.10686 = 483 seconds/mile = 8:03/mile, and 25×60 ÷ 5 = 300 seconds/km = 5:00/km.

What is an 8-minute mile pace for a 5K?

An 8:00/mile pace gives you a 5K finish time of about 24:51 — just under 25 minutes. That's a strong performance putting you in roughly the top 25% of recreational runners. The equivalent km pace is about 4:58/km.

How do I run a faster 5K?

The most effective way to improve your 5K time is to add interval training (e.g., 6×800m at goal race pace), weekly tempo runs (20 min at comfortably hard effort), and gradually increasing weekly mileage. Most runners see significant improvement in 8–12 weeks of consistent training.

About This 5K Pace Calculator

This 5K pace calculator works in three modes depending on what you already know. Time → Pace: enter your goal finish time and instantly see the pace per mile and km you need to hold. Mile Pace → Time: enter your comfortable per-mile pace to see what 5K finish time that produces. Km Pace → Time: same but starting from a per-km pace.

The 5K (5.000 km = 3.10686 miles) is the most popular race distance for recreational runners. It's long enough to test your aerobic fitness but short enough to complete on a busy day. Knowing your 5K pace is also the foundation for setting training zones, estimating 10K and half marathon times, and designing interval workouts.

Example: You want to finish in 28:30. Enter 28 min 30 sec → you need 9:10/mile (5:42/km). Your GPS watch should never show a pace faster than 9:10 for the first half of the race if you want to finish strong.

How Runners Use 5K Pace

Your 5K race pace is more than a race-day number — it's a training anchor. Coaches typically define training paces relative to current 5K fitness. Easy / recovery runs are typically 60–90 seconds slower per mile than 5K pace. Tempo runs are 20–40 seconds slower per mile — this is your lactate threshold, the pace you could theoretically sustain for about an hour. VO2max intervals are run at or slightly faster than 5K race pace, with recovery between reps. Knowing your exact 5K pace makes all these zones precise rather than approximate.

Rule of thumb: your 10K time is roughly 2× your 5K time + 2–3 minutes. Your half marathon finish is roughly 4.5× your 5K time. Your marathon finish is roughly 10× your 5K time. These are estimates; actual performance depends on weekly mileage and long-run training.
