About This 10K Pace Chart

A 10K pace chart shows the exact running pace — in minutes per mile or per km — needed to finish a 10-kilometer race at any given goal time. Whether you're targeting a personal best, chasing a sub-50 minute finish, or simply curious what pace your GPS watch should display, this chart gives you the answer instantly.

The 10K (6.214 miles) is one of the most popular race distances worldwide — long enough to reward endurance, short enough to run fast. It demands a mix of aerobic base, lactate threshold fitness, and race-day pacing discipline. This tool covers every finish time from 30 minutes (elite) to 90 minutes (beginner), in both miles and kilometers.

How to use: Enter your goal finish time in the calculator above to instantly see your per-mile and per-km pace. Or scroll the full chart to find your target row. Use the unit toggle to switch between miles-only, km-only, or both columns at once.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good 10K time?

For most recreational runners, finishing a 10K in under 60 minutes is a solid goal. Strong amateur runners aim for sub-50 minutes, while competitive club runners target sub-40 minutes. Elite runners finish in under 30 minutes. The average 10K time is around 55–65 minutes for men and 65–75 minutes for women.

How much slower is a 10K than a 5K?

Most runners are about 10–20 seconds per mile (6–12 sec/km) slower in a 10K compared to a 5K, depending on fitness level. Beginners tend to slow more, while trained runners maintain pace better. Use the 5K pace chart alongside this chart to compare your paces.

What pace do I need for a sub-50 minute 10K?

To finish a 10K in under 50 minutes, you need to maintain a pace of 8:03 per mile or 5:00 per km. This is a strong performance that requires consistent training and a solid aerobic base, including regular tempo runs and long runs.

How do I train for a 10K?

A typical 10K training plan spans 8–12 weeks and includes easy runs, tempo intervals, and a weekly long run. Key workouts are 1-mile repeats at goal 10K pace and threshold runs of 20–30 minutes. Building your weekly mileage to 25–35 miles/week is common for sub-50 minute runners.
